Immunology

Discover how the human immune system protects the body against infections, recognizes foreign substances and maintains immune balance.

Year II First Semester Pharmacy Education

📚 Course Introduction

Immunology is the scientific study of the immune system and its responses to foreign substances, microorganisms and other threats.

It explores innate and adaptive immunity, immune cells, antibodies, antigens, hypersensitivity, autoimmunity and immunological disorders.

For pharmacy students, immunology is important for understanding vaccines, immunological medicines, allergies, immune disorders and therapeutic approaches.

Learning goal: Understand the mechanisms of immune protection and their clinical importance.

Module 1: Introduction to Immunology

Learning objectives

  • Define immunology.
  • Explain the importance of the immune system.
  • Describe the major components of immunity.
  • Differentiate innate and adaptive immunity.

What is Immunology?

Immunology is the study of the body's defense system against foreign substances and disease-causing agents.

Topics

  • Spleen

  • Neutrophils

Important concept

The bone marrow and thymus are primary lymphoid organs. The lymph nodes and spleen are secondary lymphoid organs.

Module 4: Adaptive Immunity

Topics

Two major types

  • Humoral immunity: Mainly mediated by antibodies produced by B cells.

📝 Short Notes

Important Immunology Revision Notes

Innate Immunity

Innate immunity provides immediate, non-specific protection through barriers, phagocytes and other defense mechanisms.

Adaptive Immunity

Adaptive immunity is antigen-specific and develops immunological memory. It includes humoral and cell-mediated responses.

Antibodies

Antibodies are immunoglobulin proteins produced by plasma cells in response to antigens.

Immunological Memory

Memory B cells and T cells help the immune system respond more rapidly and effectively upon subsequent exposure to the same antigen.
