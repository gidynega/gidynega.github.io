Parasitology

Study medically important parasites, their structures, life cycles, transmission, diagnosis, prevention and treatment of parasitic infections.

Year II First Semester Pharmacy Education

📚 Course Introduction

Parasitology is the study of organisms that live in or on another organism (the host) and obtain benefits at the host's expense. For pharmacy students, it provides knowledge about parasites, parasitic diseases, diagnosis, prevention and antiparasitic medicines.

Learning goal: Understand important human parasites and their medical significance.

📖 Course Modules

Module 1: Introduction to Parasitology

Learning objectives

  • Define parasitology.
  • Explain host-parasite relationships.
  • Differentiate types of hosts.
  • Describe modes of parasite transmission.

Important terms

  • Parasite: An organism living in or on a host.
  • Host: An organism that harbors a parasite.
  • Definitive host: Host in which sexual reproduction or the adult stage occurs.
  • Intermediate host: Host in which larval development or asexual multiplication occurs.
  • Vector: An organism that transmits an infectious agent.

Important concept: Protozoa

Protozoa are eukaryotic, generally unicellular organisms. Some species are responsible for important human diseases.

Module 3: Helminths

Topics

  • General characteristics of helminths
  • Nematodes (roundworms)
  • Cestodes (tapeworms)
  • Trematodes (flukes)
  • Ascaris lumbricoides
  • Hookworms
  • Enterobius vermicularis
  • Taenia species
  • Schistosoma species

Classification

  • Nematodes: Cylindrical, unsegmented worms.
  • Cestodes: Flat, segmented tapeworms.
  • Trematodes: Flat, usually leaf-shaped flukes.

📝 Short Notes

Important Parasitology Revision Notes

Protozoa

Protozoa are unicellular eukaryotic organisms. Some are free-living, while others are parasitic.

Helminths

Helminths are parasitic worms, commonly classified into nematodes, cestodes and trematodes.

Malaria

Malaria is caused by Plasmodium species and is transmitted through the bite of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.

Infective Stage

The infective stage is the developmental form of a parasite capable of establishing infection in a host.
