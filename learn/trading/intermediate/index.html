COURSE 01

Technical Analysis Fundamentals

Learn how to analyze market prices and identify potential trading opportunities.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis is the study of historical price movements and trading data to evaluate market behavior and identify possible future price scenarios.

Lesson 2: Market Trends

  • Uptrend: A sequence of higher highs and higher lows.
  • Downtrend: A sequence of lower highs and lower lows.
  • Sideways trend: Price moves within a relatively horizontal range.

Lesson 3: Timeframes

A timeframe represents the duration covered by one candlestick or chart bar.

  • 1-minute chart
  • 5-minute chart
  • 15-minute chart
  • 1-hour chart
  • 4-hour chart
  • Daily chart
  • Weekly chart

Lesson 4: Multi-Timeframe Analysis

Multi-timeframe analysis means examining the same market across different timeframes to understand both broader and shorter-term price movements.

Practical Example:

A trader may examine a daily chart to understand the broader market direction and a 1-hour chart to study shorter-term price behavior.

COURSE 02

Support and Resistance

Understand important price levels and market reaction zones.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Support?

Support is a price area where buying interest has previously been strong enough to slow or reverse a downward price movement.

Lesson 2: What Is Resistance?

Resistance is a price area where selling interest has previously been strong enough to slow or reverse an upward price movement.

Lesson 3: Identifying Support and Resistance

  1. Study historical price movements.
  2. Identify areas where price repeatedly reacted.
  3. Mark the relevant price zones.
  4. Observe how price behaves when it returns.

Lesson 4: Breakouts

A breakout occurs when price moves beyond an established support or resistance area. A breakout is not automatically genuine; false breakouts can occur.

Lesson 5: Role Reversal

A previous resistance area may become support after an upward breakout. Similarly, previous support may become resistance after a downward breakout. This behavior is not guaranteed.

Practical Example:

Suppose an asset repeatedly struggles to move above $100. This area may be considered resistance. If price moves above it and later returns, observe whether buyers defend that area.

COURSE 03

Chart Patterns and Price Action

Study common chart formations and price behavior.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Are Chart Patterns?

Chart patterns are formations created by price movements that traders study to understand possible market scenarios.

Lesson 2: Head and Shoulders

The head and shoulders pattern consists of three peaks: a left shoulder, a higher middle peak called the head, and a right shoulder. It is commonly studied as a possible bearish reversal pattern.

Lesson 3: Double Top and Double Bottom

  • Double Top: Two similar peaks that may indicate weakening upward momentum.
  • Double Bottom: Two similar lows that may indicate weakening downward momentum.

Lesson 4: Triangles

  • Ascending triangle.
  • Descending triangle.
  • Symmetrical triangle.

Lesson 5: Price Action

Price action is the study of price movement itself, including candle formations, market structure, swings and reactions around important levels.

Practical Example:

If price forms two similar peaks near a resistance zone, a trader may study whether a double-top pattern is developing. Confirmation is important; the pattern alone does not guarantee a reversal.

COURSE 04

Technical Indicators

Learn how traders use mathematical indicators to study market data.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Are Technical Indicators?

Technical indicators are calculations based on price, volume or other market data. They help traders examine trends, momentum and volatility.

Lesson 2: Moving Average (MA)

A moving average calculates the average price over a selected number of periods. It can help smooth price fluctuations and highlight trends.

  • Simple Moving Average (SMA).
  • Exponential Moving Average (EMA).

Lesson 3: Relative Strength Index (RSI)

RSI is a momentum indicator that ranges from 0 to 100.

  • Above 70 is commonly described as overbought.
  • Below 30 is commonly described as oversold.

These levels are not automatic buy or sell signals. Strong trends can remain overbought or oversold.

Lesson 4: Moving Average Convergence Divergence (MACD)

MACD is a momentum and trend-following indicator based on exponential moving averages. It includes a MACD line, signal line and histogram.

Lesson 5: Bollinger Bands

Bollinger Bands consist of a middle moving average and upper and lower bands that change according to price volatility.

Practical Example:

A trader may combine a moving average with RSI to examine market direction and momentum. However, indicators can produce false signals, so they should not be treated as guarantees.

COURSE 05

Intermediate Trading Strategies

Understand how traders develop structured approaches to market analysis.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is a Trading Strategy?

A trading strategy is a structured set of rules that defines how a trader evaluates opportunities, enters positions, manages risk and exits trades.

Lesson 2: Trend-Following Strategy

Trend-following approaches attempt to participate in an established market direction.

Lesson 3: Breakout Strategy

Breakout strategies study price movement beyond an established support or resistance area. Traders may look for additional evidence before considering a breakout valid.

Lesson 4: Pullback Strategy

A pullback is a temporary movement against the broader trend. Traders study whether price may resume its previous direction.

Lesson 5: Trading Plan

A trading plan should define:
  • Markets and instruments to study.
  • Conditions required before entering a trade.
  • Entry and exit rules.
  • Risk limits and position sizing.
  • Conditions for avoiding a trade.
Practical Example:

A hypothetical trend-following plan may require an upward market structure, a pullback to a previously identified support area and additional confirmation before considering an entry.

COURSE 06

Intermediate Risk Management

Develop a deeper understanding of position sizing, risk-to-reward and trading performance.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: Risk Per Trade

Risk per trade is the amount of account equity a trader is prepared to lose if a trade reaches its planned stop-loss.

Lesson 2: Position Sizing

Position size should account for the distance between entry and stop-loss, the monetary value of price movements and the planned risk.

Calculation Example:

Account balance = $2,000
Planned risk = 1%

Risk amount = $2,000 × 0.01

Planned risk = $20

The actual position size also depends on the instrument, stop distance, contract specifications, and trading costs.

Lesson 3: Risk-to-Reward Ratio

The risk-to-reward ratio compares planned risk with potential reward.

A 1:3 ratio means the planned potential reward is three times the planned risk. It does not mean the trade will achieve that reward.

Lesson 4: Trading Journal

A trading journal records trading decisions, entry and exit prices, reasons for trades, results and lessons learned.

Lesson 5: Evaluating Trading Performance

  • Win rate.
  • Average win and average loss.
  • Profit factor.
  • Maximum drawdown.
  • Consistency of rule-following.

💡 Intermediate Study Guidelines

  • Master basic chart reading before studying complex strategies.
  • Practice identifying trends and price zones using historical charts.
  • Understand the limitations of technical indicators.
  • Keep a trading journal for simulated practice.
  • Backtest strategies before considering real-money use.
  • Never assume that a chart pattern or indicator guarantees a result.
