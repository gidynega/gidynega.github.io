01. Advanced Market Structure

Learning Objectives

Understand how price movement develops through market structure and how traders analyze changing market conditions.

Lessons

  1. Understanding market structure in greater depth.
  2. Higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL).
  3. Lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
  4. Break of Structure (BOS).
  5. Change of Character (CHoCH).
  6. Trend continuation and possible reversals.
  7. Multi-timeframe market analysis.
Example: If price repeatedly creates higher highs and higher lows, the market may be showing an uptrend. A break below an important higher low may indicate that the structure is changing.
A break of structure does not guarantee that price will continue in that direction. Always consider context and risk.

02. Liquidity and Institutional Trading Concepts

Learning Objectives

Study liquidity, order execution, and commonly discussed institutional trading concepts without assuming that chart patterns reveal the actions of specific institutions.

Lessons

  1. What is market liquidity?
  2. Buy-side and sell-side liquidity.
  3. Equal highs and equal lows.
  4. Liquidity pools and stop orders.
  5. Liquidity sweeps and false breakouts.
  6. Order blocks as a chart-analysis concept.
  7. Fair Value Gaps (FVG).
  8. Limitations of institutional trading concepts.
Example: Several similar highs may attract attention because traders sometimes place stop orders above them. Price moving above those highs and then reversing is often described as a liquidity sweep. This interpretation is not proof of who placed or executed the orders.
Terms such as order block and Fair Value Gap are analytical concepts, not guaranteed signals or proof of institutional activity.

Knowledge Check: What does liquidity generally describe?

03. Advanced Technical Analysis and Multi-Timeframe Strategy

Learning Objectives

Combine different technical-analysis methods to understand market conditions across multiple timeframes.

Lessons

  1. Top-down market analysis.
  2. Combining weekly, daily, and intraday charts.
  3. Advanced support and resistance zones.
  4. Trendlines and channels.
  5. Fibonacci retracement and extension.
  6. Volume analysis.
  7. Confluence: combining independent evidence.
  8. Creating a repeatable analysis process.
Example: A trader might first study the daily chart to understand the broader trend, then use a four-hour chart to identify important price zones, and finally use a one-hour chart to study a possible entry setup. Each timeframe provides different context.
Too many indicators can create confusion. More indicators do not automatically produce better analysis.

04. Trading Systems and Strategy Development

Learning Objectives

Learn how to build a structured trading system with clear rules that can be tested and evaluated.

Lessons

  1. What is a trading system?
  2. Defining market conditions.
  3. Entry rules and confirmation.
  4. Stop-loss and take-profit rules.
  5. Position-sizing rules.
  6. Trading setups and invalidation conditions.
  7. Writing a trading plan.
  8. Evaluating consistency and performance.
Example of a system rule: A hypothetical system may only consider a trade when the broader trend, a predefined price zone, and a specific confirmation condition are all present. It also defines in advance where the idea is invalidated.
A trading system should be tested across different market conditions. A few successful examples are not enough to demonstrate reliability.

05. Backtesting, Forward Testing and Performance Analysis

Learning Objectives

Understand how to evaluate a trading strategy using historical data and simulated trading before considering real-money use.

Lessons

  1. Introduction to backtesting.
  2. Collecting and preparing historical data.
  3. Defining test rules before testing.
  4. Win rate and average win/loss.
  5. Profit factor and expectancy.
  6. Maximum drawdown.
  7. Forward testing with a demo account.
  8. Overfitting and look-ahead bias.
  9. Keeping accurate performance records.
Example: Suppose a strategy has 40 winning trades with an average gain of $30 and 60 losing trades with an average loss of $10.

Gross profit = 40 × $30 = $1,200

Gross loss = 60 × $10 = $600

Profit factor = $1,200 ÷ $600 = 2.0

This simplified example excludes fees, spreads, slippage, and other trading costs.

Past performance does not guarantee future results. A strategy can perform differently when market conditions change.

06. Professional Risk Management and Portfolio Protection

Learning Objectives

Study advanced methods of controlling risk at the trade, account, and portfolio levels.

Lessons

  1. Risk of ruin and account preservation.
  2. Position sizing and percentage risk.
  3. Portfolio exposure and correlated positions.
  4. Risk-to-reward and expectancy.
  5. Maximum daily and weekly loss limits.
  6. Leverage and margin risks.
  7. Trading during high-volatility events.
  8. Building a risk-management policy.
Position-sizing example: If a hypothetical account is $1,000 and the planned risk is 1%, the maximum planned loss is:

$1,000 × 0.01 = $10

This is a risk calculation, not a recommendation to risk 1% or to open a trade. Actual losses may exceed planned amounts because of gaps, slippage, and execution conditions.

Leverage magnifies both gains and losses. Some products can cause losses greater than the initial amount deposited.

07. Advanced Trading Psychology and Decision-Making

Learning Objectives

Develop disciplined decision-making and understand the psychological challenges that can affect trading behavior.

Lessons

  1. Emotional control and self-awareness.
  2. Fear of missing out (FOMO).
  3. Revenge trading.
  4. Overtrading and impulsive decisions.
  5. Confirmation bias and overconfidence.
  6. Following a written trading plan.
  7. Reviewing mistakes without emotional judgment.
  8. Building consistent routines.
Example: After experiencing a loss, a trader may feel pressure toimmediately enter another trade to recover the money. Recognizing this emotional reaction and following a predefined trading plan can help prevent impulsive decisions.

Knowledge Check: What is revenge trading?

Advanced Learning Guidelines

  • Study one course at a time.
  • Practice chart analysis using historical charts.
  • Use demo or simulated trading for practice.
  • Keep a trading journal and evaluate your decisions.
  • Test strategies before considering real-money trading.
  • Never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
