COURSE 01

Introduction to Trading

Understand trading, financial markets and essential terminology.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Trading?

Trading is the activity of buying and selling financial assets with the aim of benefiting from price movements. Financial assets include currencies, stocks, commodities and other instruments.

Lesson 2: How Trading Works

Trading involves buyers, sellers, financial markets and intermediaries such as brokers. Prices change as supply and demand change.

Lesson 3: Types of Financial Markets

  • Forex Market: Exchange of currencies.
  • Stock Market: Buying and selling company shares.
  • Commodity Market: Trading commodities such as gold and oil.
  • Cryptocurrency Market: Trading digital assets.

Lesson 4: Important Trading Terms

  • Asset: Something that can be traded.
  • Broker: A company that provides access to trading markets.
  • Buy: Opening a position to benefit from a price increase.
  • Sell: Opening a position to benefit from a price decrease.
  • Profit: Financial gain from a trade.
  • Loss: Financial loss from a trade.
Practical Example:

Suppose an asset is priced at $100. You buy one unit and later sell it for $110. Your gross profit is $10 before fees and other costs.

COURSE 02

Financial Markets and Forex Fundamentals

Learn about currency pairs, market movements and Forex terminology.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Forex?

Forex means foreign exchange. It is the global market where currencies are exchanged against one another.

Lesson 2: Currency Pairs

Forex currencies are quoted in pairs. For example, EUR/USD represents the euro against the US dollar.

  • Base currency: The first currency in a pair.
  • Quote currency: The second currency in a pair.
  • Major pairs: Commonly traded pairs involving the US dollar.
  • Cross pairs: Currency pairs that do not include the US dollar.

Lesson 3: Bid and Ask Prices

  • Bid: The price at which a buyer can sell to the market.
  • Ask: The price at which a buyer can purchase from the market.
  • Spread: The difference between the ask and bid prices.

Lesson 4: Pips and Lots

A pip is a standard unit used to describe changes in exchange rates. A lot is a standardized trading volume. Pip values depend on the currency pair, position size and account currency.

Example:

If EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1010, the movement is 10 pips under the conventional four-decimal pip quotation.

COURSE 03

Candlesticks and Chart Basics

Understand price charts and the information represented by candlesticks.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is a Trading Chart?

A trading chart is a visual representation of an asset's price movement over a selected period.

Lesson 2: Types of Charts

  • Line Chart
  • Bar Chart
  • Candlestick Chart

Lesson 3: Understanding Candlesticks

A candlestick displays four important prices:

  • Open: Price at the beginning of the period.
  • High: Highest price during the period.
  • Low: Lowest price during the period.
  • Close: Price at the end of the period.

Lesson 4: Bullish and Bearish Candles

  • Bullish candle: Closing price is above opening price.
  • Bearish candle: Closing price is below opening price.

Lesson 5: Understanding Wicks and Bodies

The candle body represents the distance between opening and closing prices. The upper and lower wicks show the highest and lowest prices reached.

Practical Example:

If a candle opens at 100 and closes at 105, it is bullish. If it opens at 105 and closes at 100, it is bearish.

COURSE 04

Risk Management Fundamentals

Learn how to understand trading risks and protect your capital.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Risk Management?

Risk management is the process of identifying, measuring and controlling potential financial losses.

Lesson 2: Capital Protection

Protecting capital is an important part of responsible trading. A trader should understand how much money could be lost before opening a position.

Lesson 3: Stop-Loss Orders

A stop-loss order is designed to close a position when the market reaches a specified price. Execution can differ from the requested price during market gaps or rapid movements.

Lesson 4: Position Sizing

Position sizing means determining how large a trading position should be based on the account size, acceptable risk and trade setup.

Practical Example:

Suppose a hypothetical trading account has $1,000. If a trader chooses to risk 1% on a trade, the planned risk is $10.

Calculation: $1,000 × 0.01 = $10.

Lesson 5: Risk-to-Reward Ratio

The risk-to-reward ratio compares the amount potentially lost with the amount potentially gained. For example, a 1:2 ratio means risking $10 for a potential gain of $20.

COURSE 05

Introduction to Trading Psychology

Understand emotions, patience, discipline and responsible decision-making.

📖 Open Course Materials

Lesson 1: What Is Trading Psychology?

Trading psychology refers to the emotions, thoughts and behaviors that influence trading decisions.

Lesson 2: Common Trading Emotions

  • Fear: Anxiety about possible losses.
  • Greed: Excessive desire for profits.
  • Hope: Expecting a losing trade to recover without a sound plan.
  • Frustration: Emotional reactions after losses.

Lesson 3: Discipline and Patience

Discipline means following a well-defined learning or trading plan. Patience means waiting for suitable conditions rather than making impulsive decisions.

Lesson 4: Avoiding Emotional Trading

  • Avoid revenge trading after losses.
  • Do not trade simply because of excitement.
  • Keep a trading journal.
  • Review decisions objectively.
  • Never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
